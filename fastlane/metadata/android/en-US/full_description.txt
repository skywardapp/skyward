Skyward tells you, ahead of time, about natural events worth seeing from
where you are — or from where you'd be willing to travel.

"A total solar eclipse crosses 180 km south of you next August — here's when
to leave." "Kp is forecast at 6 tonight; aurora may be visible from your
latitude." "Perseids peak Thursday night, new moon, radiant high after
23:00."

FEATURES

* Solar and lunar eclipses, computed on-device — exact timing, obscuration,
  and (for total/annular eclipses) the path of totality, with no network
  access required.
* Aurora alerts from NOAA's Kp forecast and OVATION nowcast, adjusted for
  your geomagnetic latitude.
* Meteor showers, using the Stellarium project's shower catalog, with
  moonlight and radiant-altitude aware viewing quality.
* Comets, from NASA/JPL orbital elements propagated on-device — predicted
  magnitude, sky position, and best viewing window.
* Supermoons and planetary conjunctions.
* A fully custom reminder rule engine: compose your own conditions (how
  close, how bright, how good a viewing quality) and notification schedule
  per phenomenon, or use the sensible defaults.
* "Where's the nearest place I *can* see this, and how far is that?" — every
  phenomenon answers the travel question, not just the "visible from home"
  question.

PRIVACY

Skyward is local-only: no account, no hosted backend, no telemetry. The only
network traffic is direct HTTPS calls from your device to public data
providers (NOAA, NASA) to fetch aurora, comet, and terrestrial-event data —
everything else (eclipses, meteor showers, moon events, sky positions) is
computed entirely on-device. Your saved locations never leave your device.
